Joint and combined variations

In some activities, one variable can depend on several other variables to operate effectively. Such relationships are described by joint and combined variations. Engage in Activity 4.5 to explore more about joint and combined variations in real-life activities.

Joint variation

Consider a formula for finding the area of a triangle. It is given by

Area=12×base×height.

In this equation, the area of a triangle varies directly as the product of its base and height. Variable relationships of this nature are known as joint variations. This specific variation can be generally expressed as

Area∝base×height.

A joint variation occurs when a variable is directly or inversely proportional to the product of two or more variables. Mathematically, if a variable z varies jointly as x and y, the general formula for joint variation can be written as:
