Combined variations

Consider the formula used to evaluate a person’s body mass in relation to his/her height. The value obtained is called Body Mass Index (BMI) and it is given by the formula

Body Mass Index (BMI)=massheight2

In this formula, BMI varies directly with mass and inversely with the square of the height of the person. This relationship is known as a combined variation.

Thus, combined variation involves a variable that varies both jointly and inversely with other variables. That is, it is a combination of direct and inverse variations.

Example 1.14

If x varies directly as y and inversely as z, and x=8 when y=12 and z=6, find the value of x when y=16 and z=4.

Solution

If x varies directly as y and inversely proportional as z, that is, x∝y and x∝1z. The combined variation becomes x∝yz. The variation equation is given by;

x=kyz.

It implies that

x1z1y1=x2z2y2=k

Given x1=8, y1=12, z1=6, y2=16, and z2=4, then

x2=x1z1y2y1z2
=8×6×1612×4
= 16

Therefore, the value of x is 16.

Example 1.15

Three tailors can sew 15 clothes in 5 days. How long will it take 5 tailors working at the same rate to sew 20 clothes?

Solution

Let t, d and c represent the number of tailors, days, and clothes, respectively.

The number of tailors is inversely proportional to the number of days, that is, t∝1d and is directly proportional to the number of clothes, that is, t∝c. Combining the two proportions gives,

t∝cd.

The variation equation is then given as

t=kcd

The value of k is computed as follows:

Given t=3, d=5, and c=15,

k=tdc
=3×515
= 1

So, the variation equation is t=cd.
