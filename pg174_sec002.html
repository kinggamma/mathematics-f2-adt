Trigonometric ratios of special angles

Special angles are angles whose trigonometric ratios can be found by using simple ratios. The special angles are 30∘, 45∘, 60∘, and 90∘.

Consider the equilateral triangle ABC in Figure 8.3. Let the length of each side be 2 units. The altitude from vertex A bisects BC at D.

From Figure 8.3, it follows that

BD=DC=1 unit.

Apply the Pythagoras' theorem:

BD2+AD2=AB2
12+AD2=22
AD2=4−1=3
AD=3 units.

Thus, the following trigonometric ratios are obtained:

sin⁡60∘=ADAB=32,
tan⁡60∘=ADBD=31=3,
cos⁡60∘=BDAB=12,
sin⁡30∘=BDAB=12,
tan⁡30∘=BDAD=13=33,
cos⁡30∘=ADAB=32.

Consider an isosceles triangle PQR in Figure 8.4 in which the base angle is 45° and PQ=RQ=1 unit.

cos⁡45∘=PQPR=12=22.
