Example 6.26

The formula for determining a compound interest is given by A=P(1+r)^t, where A is the final amount, P is the principal, r is the annual interest rate, and t is the time in years. Find the number of years it will take for the principal to double if r = 5%.

Solution

Given A=2P,\ r=5\%,\ A=P(1+r)^t From A=P(1+r)^t, substitute the given values to get,

2P=P(1+0.05)^t
2=(1.05)^t

Apply logarithms on both sides and simplify to obtain,

log⁡2=log⁡(1.05)t
log⁡2=tlog⁡(1.05)
t=log⁡2log⁡(1.05)

Using a calculator to find the value of t gives t = 14.2.

Therefore, the principal will double after 14.2 years.

=1012
