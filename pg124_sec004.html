Standard form of numbers

When a number is expressed in the form A×10n, where 1≤A<10 and n is an integer, it is said to be in standard form or scientific notation or standard notation. For instance, the following numbers are expressed in standard form:

(a)
290=2.9×100=2.9×102
(b)
29=2.9×10=2.9×101
(c)
2.9=2.9×100
(d)
0.29=2.9×0.1=2.9×10−1

When writing numbers in standard form, the following must be considered:

1.

For numbers between 0 and 1, move the decimal point towards right until a number between 1 and 10 is obtained and the exponent of 10 is negative.

2.

For numbers greater than or equal to 10, move the decimal point left until the value is between 1 and 10, resulting in a positive exponent of 10.

3.

The exponent of 10 is determined by the number of places the decimal point is moved, either to the right or left.
