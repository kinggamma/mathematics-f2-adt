From Activity 7.3, one may have learned that, the number of elements of a set can be obtained by counting elements in the given set. The number of elements of a set A is denoted by n(A) and read as “the number of elements in set A”.

For any two sets A and B, the following are the relationships between the two sets.

1.
n(A∪B)=n(A)+n(B)-n(A∩B)
2.
n(μ)=n(A)+n(A′)
3.
n(A)=n(A∩B′)+n(A∩B)
4.
n(A′)=n(A′∩B)+n(A′∩B′)

Example 7.14

A total of 40 people attending a seminar were asked to describe the source of energy between gas and charcoal they use in cooking. Among them, 16 said they use gas, 25 use charcoal and 6 use neither gas nor charcoal. How many participants use both gas and charcoal?

Solution

Let μ={people attended the seminar}, G={people who use gas}, and C={people who use charcoal}.

Thus, n(μ)=40, n(G)=16, n(C)=25, and n((G∪C)′)=6.

Required to find n(G∩C).

From n(G∪C)=n(μ)-n((G∪C)′).

40-6=34

But n(G∪C)=n(G)+n(C)-n(G∩C). Rearranging the equation gives,

n(G∩C)=n(G)+n(C)-n(G∪C)
n(G∩C)=16+25-34
=7

Therefore, 7 participants used both gas and charcoal.

Example 7.15

In a community of 500 people, 300 are in the climate action group and 250 are in a poverty alleviation group. If 180 are in both groups, how many people are in each of the following categories?

(a) Climate action group only.

(b) Poverty alleviation group only.

(c) At least in one group.

(d) Neither of the two groups.

Solution

Let: μ={people in a community}
C={people in climate action group}
P={people in poverty alleviation group}

Given n(μ)=500, n(C)=300, n(P)=250, and n(C∩P)=180.

(a) Required to find n(C∩P′).

From n(C)=n(C∩P)+n(C∩P′).

Thus, 300=180+n(C∩P′).

n(C∩P′)=300-180
=120

Therefore, 120 people are only in climate action group.

(b) Required to find n(C′∩P).

n(P)=n(C∩P)+n(C′∩P).

n(C′∩P)=250-180
=70

Therefore, 70 people are in poverty alleviation group.

(c) Required to find n(C∪P).

n(C∪P)=n(C)+n(P)-n(C∩P)
=300+250-180
=370

Therefore, 370 people are at least in one group.

(d) Required to find n((C∪P)′).

n(μ)=n(C∪P)+n((C∪P)′)

500=370+n((C∪P)′)

n((C∪P)′)=500-370

=130

Therefore, 130 people are in neither of the two groups.

Example 7.16

In a village with 400 households, 240 have access to clean water and 220 have good sanitation. If 90 households have no access to clean water and good sanitation, how many households have:
(a) Both clean water and good sanitation.
(b) Only one of these services.

Solution

Let μ={households in a village},
W={households with access to clean water},
S={households with good sanitation}.

Given n(μ)=400, n(W)=240, n(S)=220, and n((W∪S)′)=90.

(a) Required to find n(W∩S).

n(μ)=n(W∪S)+n((W∪S)′)

400=n(W∪S)+90

n(W∪S)=400-90

=310

But n(W∪S)=n(W)+n(S)-n(W∩S)

310=240+220-n(W∩S)

n(W∩S)=240+220-310

=460−310

=150

Therefore, 150 households have both clean water and good sanitation.

(b) n(W)=n(W∩S)+n(W∩S′)

240=150+n(W∩S′)

n(W∩S′)=240-150

=90 (have clean water only)

But n(S)=n(W∩S)+n(W′∩S).

220=150+n(W′∩S)

n(W′∩S)=220-150

=70 (have sanitation only)

Households with only one service = clean water only + sanitation only

=90+70

=160

Therefore, 160 have only one of these services.
