Example 3.9

With reasons, identify a pair of triangles which are similar in each of the following triangles.

△XYZ has side lengths 4, 6, and 8.

△PQR has side lengths 10, 16, and 8.

△EFG has side lengths 10, 15, and 20.

Solution

To show that the triangles are similar, find the ratio of the lengths of corresponding sides (shortest, longest and the remaining sides).

Comparing △XYZ and △PQR: Ratio of shortest sides: XYPR=48=12 Ratio of longest sides: XZQR=816=12 Ratio of remaining sides: YZPQ=610=35 The ratios of the corresponding sides are not all equal. Therefore △XYZ and △PQR are not similar.

Comparing △XYZ and △EFG. Considering △XYZ and △PQR: Ratio of shortest sides: XYEF=410=25

Ratio of longest sides: XZEG=820=25 Ratio of remaining sides: YZFG=615=25 The ratios of the corresponding sides are equal. Therefore, △XYZ∼△PQR. Comparing △PQR and △EFG. Ratio of shortest sides: PREF=810=45 Ratio of longest sides: QREG=1620=45 Ratio of remaining sides: PQFG=1015=23 The ratios of the corresponding sides are not all equal. Therefore, △PQR and △EFG are not similar.

Side-Angle-Side (SAS) similarity theorem

The SAS similarity theorem states that, two triangles are similar if two pairs of their corresponding sides are proportional and one pair of corresponding angles (formed by these sides) are equal. Figure 3.5 describes the SAS theorem.
