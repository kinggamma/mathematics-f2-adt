Laws of logarithms

The laws of logarithms, also known as logarithm rules, are fundamental identities or rules that describe how to manipulate logarithms.

The following are the key laws:

1. Product ruleloga⁡(xy)=loga⁡x+loga⁡y
2. Quotient ruleloga⁡(xy)=loga⁡x−loga⁡y
3. Power rule or Rule of exponentsloga⁡mn=nloga⁡m
4. Roots rule
loga⁡xnm=loga⁡xnm=nmloga⁡xwhere n and m are integers and m ≠ 0
5. Change of base formulaloga⁡x=logc⁡xlogc⁡a

Derivation of the laws of logarithms

The laws of logarithms can be derived as follows:

Product rule

Let p=loga⁡x and q=loga⁡y

(1)

Expressing equation (1) in exponential form gives,

x=ap and y=aq

(2)

From equation (2), multiply x by y to obtain

xy=ap×aq.

(3)

Expressing equation (3) in logarithmic form gives,

loga⁡(xy)=loga⁡ap+q

=(p+q)loga⁡a

=p+q

Thus, loga⁡(xy)=p+q.

(4)

Substituting equation (1) into equation (4) gives

loga⁡(xy)=loga⁡x+loga⁡y.

Therefore, loga⁡(xy)=loga⁡x+loga⁡y.
